## Making a case for the drive bays

The NorthStar Advantage has two drive bays, and I want to use them with a Gotek floppy-drive emulator. A Gotek is a device that can stand in for a floppy disk drive. It uses a USB flash drive to provide disk images instead of physical floppy disks using a USB stick.

The original drives belong to a period when 5¼-inch floppy disks were common. Shugart Associates helped make this drive size popular in the 70s-80s. These drives mattered because they gave personal computers a practical way to load and save programs. The old drives in my Advantage are part of its history, but an emulator can make it easier to use without relying on aging disks and drive parts.

I've maintained always a preference for using original hardware when possible, but in this case, due to difficulties in locate disk media, the Gotek emulator provides a practical solution for keeping the system functional.

I found a wonderful 3D designing tool, I have asked a friend to print it for me, to fit it in the Advantage’s drive bays. The pieces in this photo include flat panels, side walls, holes and slots. They have visible print layers, but look intact.

A 3D-printed case gives me a way to make a shape that fits the computer and the gotek. It also lets me place openings for the controls and connectors. I do not have a printer model, print settings, or exact case measurements to share here.

## The Gotek board and its controls

I bought the Gotek board separately, with oled display included and rotary encoder for control. The rotary encoder allows for navigating menus and selecting options on the display, providing a user-friendly interface for managing disk images.

![Open Gotek emulator board wired inside a black drive enclosure.](https://i.imgur.com/0r5uJd2.jpeg)

The Gotek’s original firmware is the software that controls how the device behaves. I have replaced the firmware as part of this project. Different firmware can change how an emulator handles disk images and controls. I need one able to emulate the hard coded sector layout of the original floppy drives used in the NorthStar Advantage.

FlashFloppy is a popular replacement firmware for Gotek floppy emulators. It allows the emulator to handle a wider range of disk image formats and provides more control over how the device interacts with vintage computers. By using FlashFloppy, I can ensure compatibility with the NorthStar Advantage’s original floppy drive sector layout.
