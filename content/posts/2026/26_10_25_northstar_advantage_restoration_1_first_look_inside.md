## A restoration that took more than a year

I bought this NorthStar Advantage on Wallapop in 2024. The seller told me that the keyboard had a fault. But at power-on, the computer still displayed a message on the screen. As always trust in the seller’s words, was an act of faith.

Indeed, buying vintage computers is always a bit of a gamble but be prepared for surprises when dealing with vintage hardware.

Vintage Hardware is not difficult to work with, usually is not difficult find information and there are a lot of resources available online for troubleshooting and repairs. Parts may be harder to find, but with patience and persistence, it is often possible to locate what is needed.

The restoration took longer than expected, but it was a great journey to bring this vintage machine back to life. I finished in 2025, but only now have I had time to document it properly.

The Advantage is an early-1980s computer made by North Star Computers. It followed the company’s earlier Horizon system, but put the computer, screen, keyboard, and two floppy drives in one case. That all-in-one design made it a complete workstation in a single unit. Its graphics were also an important feature for the time.

This computer appeared around 1982 in the early 1980s as a high-end personal computer with integrated graphics and storage. Was the front image of the by at that time annual computer catalog.

![Catalog image of the NorthStar Advantage computer.](/images/unsorted/guia_1983.jpg)


## What the Advantage could do

The North Star Advantage used a Z80A processor running at 4 MHz. The system had 64 KB of user memory and 16 KB of graphics memory. 

The built-in monochrome screen was 11 inches. It could show 80-column text or graphics at 640 by 200 pixels. This made it suitable for more than text and business programs. North Star supplied four demonstration programs, including one that drew patterns of lines.

The system used CP/M with graphical extensions. CP/M was an operating system used by many early personal computers. North Star also supplied a version of its own DOS, called Graphics DOS, or GDOS, with graphics support. The computer had six expansion slots. These could accept cards for features such as serial or parallel connections, or an additional processor.

North Star also offered an optional 8088 processor, but not as an option, it was an additional processor board. The intel processor allowed the Advantage to run MS-DOS 1.0.

A 5 MB Winchester disk was another optional feature. “Winchester” was a name used for an early type of fixed hard disk.

The Advantage was sold in June 1982 for £2,295 plus VAT.

## Keyboard

The keyboard needs attention. I opened it up and removed the keycaps, exposing the switch housings and the circuit assembly.

![Vintage keyboard with exposed circuit board and ribbon cable on gray fabric.](https://i.imgur.com/xHiZTbS.jpeg)

The underside of the keyboard circuit board has printed traces in good condition, numbered positions and screws. Some of the printing is hard to make out.

![Underside of a keyboard circuit board with green traces and ribbon cable](https://i.imgur.com/XwQeVXn.jpeg)

![Close-up of a numbered keyboard circuit board with green traces and screws.](https://i.imgur.com/sgyy8wD.jpeg)

There is also a multipin connector attached to a gray ribbed cable that connects the keyboard to the main board.

![Blue multipin connector attached to a gray ribbed cable on carpet.](https://i.imgur.com/L7SwENa.jpeg)

With the keycaps off, the dust and grime around the switches are easier to see. The keyboard is still in the disassembly and cleaning stage. It uses foam and foil capacitive keys. As is usually the case with these 30+ year old keyboards, the foam pads have deteriorated. This type of keyboard often needs new foam pads to work properly again.

### Faulty Foam Pads

The seller mentioned that the keyboard wasn´t in working condition, and I see the problem, which is the deteriorated foam pad, this is a common issue with vintage capacitive keyboards, and it is something that will need to be addressed during the restoration process.

![Close-up of a dirty keyboard with keycaps removed.](https://i.imgur.com/MXNBk5b.jpeg)

![Disassembled dirty vintage keyboard with its circuit membrane exposed](https://i.imgur.com/9foPGMG.jpeg)

![Exposed, dusty keyboard circuit board with ribbon cable and key contacts.](https://i.imgur.com/e6hDCE6.jpeg)

The removed keycaps are laid out below the keyboard alongside dust and debris.

![Disassembled keyboard with exposed switches and scattered keycaps during cleaning.](https://i.imgur.com/tgIHRtV.jpeg)

Because this is a capacitive keyboard, it is particularly sensitive to the condition of the foam pads. Proper cleaning and replacement of the foam pads are essential steps in the restoration process.

## LOAD SYSTEM

The good news is that I can type on the keyboard, even though the foam pads have deteriorated. This indicates that the basic functionality of the keys is still intact, and with proper cleaning and replacement of the foam pads, the keyboard should be fully operational again.

While the keyboard is undergoing cleaning and restoration, the CRT is showing repeated “LOAD SYSTEM” text, with a green block cursor below it. It was a good sign that the machine could still display this message despite the keyboard issues.
